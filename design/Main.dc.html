Design brief

Three directions for the report reader

Pick one. Each explores a different idea of what this product is, not a different shade of the same idea.

What is wrong today

The current site is cream #FAF8F3 with a Fraunces display face, Inter body, and a teal accent, laid out as a centred hero above a stack of rounded cards.

Nothing there is badly made. The problem is that it is the default: that exact palette and that exact type pairing are what every generated site looks like right now. It reads as competent and anonymous, which is why it has no lift. It also says nothing about what makes this product unusual.

What is actually distinctive

01It reads a document you already own and hands it back to you readable.
02Eighteen languages, twelve of them Indic. Almost nothing in health does this.
03Every printed value survives, with its range, even when the explanation fails.

The three directions

A — Facing page

A bilingual edition of your report. What the paper says on the left, what it means on the right. Signature: the reference range drawn as a real scale with your value plotted on it.

Tradeoff: quiet and grown-up, but the least immediately striking.

B — Eighteen

The languages are the hero. One sentence, set at display size, arriving script by script. Signature: a typographic wall no competitor can copy without doing the same work.

Tradeoff: the loudest and most memorable, and the most likely to feel loud in a clinic.

C — Instrument

A calm dark reading instrument. Sixty values scannable at a glance. Signature: one continuous strip chart where the outliers find you instead of you hunting them.

Tradeoff: excellent for a dense panel, colder for someone frightened by their result.
